(Basic) Philosophies of technology

  • instrumentalism (naive)
  • determinism (hard, soft, substantivism)

Instrumentalism

  • Technology is a neutral tool – it is neither good nor bad
  • Technology does not contain inherent moral values
  • Humans have complete control over how it is used
  • High human agency – society decides whether technology is used for good or bad

“Guns don’t kill people, people kill people.”

Determinism

  • Technology causes/drives social changes
  • Technology shapes human behavior and society
  • Low human agency – humans adapt to technology (we cannot easily stop its trajectory)
  • Determinism (technology is autonomous and neutral) vs. Substantivism (technology is autonomous and value-laden)

“Cellphones have ruined face-to-face interactions”

Instrumentalism vs. Determinism

Humans control technology

Technology is neutral

Technology controls or shapes society

Soft technological determinism (Critical theory)

  • Technology both shapes society and is shaped by it
  • Technology and social practices develop in tandem and influence one another
  • Technology is the means, human purposes are the end/goal
  • Some room for human agency – engineers can inscribe their intentions within technology to shape human behavior
  • Awareness of unintended social or environmental consequences

Technological constructivism

  • Social construction of technology (SCOT)
  • Technology does not determine human action, human action shapes technology
  • Technology is embedded within its respective social context
  • Humans accept or reject technology

Summary

  • Instrumentalism: Humans control technology, technology is neutral
  • Determinism: Technology causes/drives social changes
  • Soft technological determinism: Technology shapes society and is shaped by it (engineers can imbue their values into technology, unintended consequences are often present)
  • Technological constructivism: social context shapes technology

One implication of the interactional stance is that “technology is neither good nor bad; nor is it neutral” (Kranzberg, 1986). Thus, design matters. That is, values can be embodied, at least to some extent, within the features of a tool or technology.

Policies

Where lawmakers fall in the deterministic and instrumentalist spectrum reflects in their (our) policies:

  • Solutionism – lawmakers believe technology will solve all the world’s problems
  • Fatalism – lawmakers have resigned themselves to the consequences of technology
  • Regulation – lawmaker believe technology should be regulated

Policies

Thinking and acting more critically towards technology enables societies to escape merely adapting to technological advances asserted as inevitable. – Andrew Smith (2026)
